AtlasDesk Platform Overview

AtlasDesk is a fictional customer-support platform used by mid-sized
software companies. It has an Inbox for customer conversations, a
Knowledge Hub for approved information, and an Operations Console for
administrative settings. Each customer organization receives its own
workspace, and every workspace has exactly one primary region that
determines where conversation content is stored.

The platform distinguishes a conversation, a ticket, and an incident. A
conversation is the exchange between a customer and the support
organization. A ticket is a tracked work item that can contain one or
more conversations. An incident is an operational event affecting the
availability or behavior of the platform. A single incident may be
linked to hundreds of tickets, but a ticket is never converted into an
incident; instead, the ticket is linked to the incident record.

Conversations arrive through four channels: email, web chat, the in-app
widget, and the public API. The channel is recorded on the conversation
and cannot be changed later. When a customer replies to an old email
thread after the related ticket has been resolved for more than 14 days,
AtlasDesk opens a new ticket instead of reopening the old one, and it
links the two tickets so agents can see the history.

Workspace regions

A workspace can be hosted in the North America, Europe, or Asia-Pacific
region. The primary region is chosen during workspace creation. Region
migration is possible but requires a scheduled maintenance window of up
to six hours, during which the Inbox is read-only. Attachments are
migrated after conversation text, so an attachment can appear as pending
migration for up to 24 hours after the window closes. Knowledge articles
are not region-bound; they are replicated to every region so that
GuideFlow can retrieve them with low latency.

GuideFlow

AtlasDesk uses a retrieval-assisted answer system called GuideFlow.
GuideFlow first normalizes the user's message, then retrieves candidate
passages from approved knowledge, ranks the candidates, and gives the
highest-ranked eligible passages to an answer generator. If no passage
clears the minimum relevance threshold, GuideFlow asks an agent to
review the question rather than inventing an answer.

Normalization removes signatures, quoted reply chains, and tracking
footers from email messages. It also expands a small list of product
abbreviations, so OC becomes Operations Console and KH becomes Knowledge
Hub. Normalization does not translate messages. A message written in
Portuguese is retrieved against Portuguese passages unless the workspace
has enabled cross-language retrieval.

The default minimum relevance threshold is 0.55. Administrators can
raise the threshold to make GuideFlow more cautious, but they cannot set
it below 0.40. Raising the threshold increases the number of questions
routed to agents. Lowering it increases coverage but also increases the
risk that a loosely related passage is used to answer a question it does
not actually address.

Knowledge articles have four states: Draft, Review, Approved, and
Archived. Draft articles are editable. Review articles are waiting for
supervisor approval. Approved articles may be used for customer-facing
answer generation. Archived articles remain available to administrators
for historical reference but are excluded from normal customer-facing
retrieval.

The retrieval system also uses metadata filters. A passage can have a
very high semantic similarity score and still be excluded if its article
is Archived, if it belongs to the wrong product area, or if it fails a
language requirement. For example, an Archived passage with a similarity
score of 0.84 can lose to an Approved passage with a score of 0.79
because eligibility is checked before final selection.

Answer generation uses at most three passages. If more than three
passages are eligible, GuideFlow keeps the three highest-ranked passages
and discards the rest, even when the discarded passages come from the
same article. Every generated answer includes citations to the passages
it used. An answer without at least one citation is never shown to a
customer; it is shown to the agent as a draft instead.

GuideFlow records retrieval diagnostics. A diagnostic record contains a
query identifier, article identifiers considered during retrieval,
selected passage identifiers, and a confidence band. When redaction mode
is enabled, the diagnostic record does not contain the customer's raw
message. This means an administrator can investigate retrieval behavior
without necessarily seeing the original conversation text.

Confidence bands are High, Medium, and Low. High means the top passage
cleared the threshold by at least 0.15. Medium means it cleared the
threshold by less than 0.15. Low is used only for agent-facing drafts,
because a Low answer is never sent automatically. Confidence bands
describe retrieval, not the factual accuracy of the generated answer.

Roles

The platform has three default roles. Agents can work tickets and use
approved knowledge. Supervisors can approve knowledge articles and
review team analytics. Administrators can change workspace configuration
such as retention, billing, and access settings. A supervisor does not
automatically become an administrator merely because the supervisor can
approve an article.

Custom roles can be created from any default role. A custom role starts
with the permissions of the role it was copied from, and later changes
to the original default role are not propagated to the copy. This is why
two custom roles that were copied from the Supervisor role at different
times can behave differently.

AtlasDesk separates customer-facing knowledge from operational state. A
billing record can explain why a charge occurred, while an approved
knowledge article can explain that charge to a customer. GuideFlow
therefore retrieves from the knowledge layer first and accesses
operational data only through explicitly configured integrations.
