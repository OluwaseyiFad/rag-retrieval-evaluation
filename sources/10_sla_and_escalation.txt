AtlasDesk Service Levels and Escalation

Support SLA targets

AtlasDesk measures support performance against service level agreement
targets. The targets depend on the ticket priority and the customer's
plan.

First-response targets:
- Urgent: 1 hour on Enterprise, 2 hours on Growth, 8 hours on Starter.
- High: 4 hours on Enterprise, 8 hours on Growth, 1 business day on
  Starter.
- Normal: 1 business day on all plans.
- Low: 3 business days on all plans.

Resolution targets are only defined for Enterprise: 1 business day for
Urgent and 3 business days for High. Growth and Starter have no
resolution target, although resolution time is still measured and
reported.

SLA timers

The first-response timer starts when the ticket is created and stops at
the first human response. The resolution timer runs while the ticket is
Open and stops while the ticket is Pending or On Hold. Because GuideFlow
answers do not count as human responses, an automated answer does not
stop the first-response timer.

Business days are counted in the workspace's support time zone, which
can differ from the billing time zone. Public holidays are excluded only
if the workspace has uploaded a holiday calendar.

Priority changes

If a ticket's priority is raised, the first-response target is
recalculated from the ticket creation time, not from the time of the
change. This means raising a ticket to Urgent can immediately put it in
breach. If priority is lowered, the new, longer target applies, but a
breach that already happened is not removed from reports.

Breaches and escalation

When a ticket is within 20 percent of its first-response target without
a response, it is highlighted in the Inbox and the assigned team's
supervisor is notified. When the target is breached, the ticket is
escalated to the team supervisor and recorded as a breach in team
analytics.

Repeated breaches trigger a service review. If a single team breaches
more than 5 percent of Urgent tickets in a calendar month, the
supervisor must submit a remediation plan within 10 business days. A
service review is not the same as a post-incident review: a service
review is about support performance, while a post-incident review is
about a platform incident.

Service credits

Enterprise contracts may include service credits for platform
availability. If monthly availability falls below 99.9 percent, the
customer can request a service credit of 10 percent of the monthly
subscription subtotal. Below 99.0 percent, the credit is 25 percent.
Service credits must be requested within 30 days after the end of the
affected month and are applied as adjustments on a later invoice.
Service credits are not paid out as refunds. Support SLA breaches do not
generate service credits; only platform availability does.
