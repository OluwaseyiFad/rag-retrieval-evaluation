AtlasDesk Knowledge Hub and Search

The AtlasDesk Knowledge Hub organizes information into articles,
collections, and passages. An article is the authoring unit, a
collection groups related articles, and a passage is the retrievable
unit used by GuideFlow. One article can therefore produce several
passages during indexing.

Publication workflow

An article follows a publication workflow. In Draft, the author can edit
it. In Review, it is waiting for supervisor approval. In Approved, it
can be used for customer-facing answer generation. Archived articles are
retained for historical reference but excluded from normal
customer-facing retrieval.

The approval workflow has two checks. First, the article owner confirms
that the content is factually correct and complete. Second, a supervisor
confirms that it is appropriate for customer-facing use. Passing the
first check does not publish the article. It remains in Review until the
supervisor approves it.

If a supervisor rejects an article in Review, it returns to Draft with
the rejection comment attached. Editing an Approved article creates a
new Draft version while the previous Approved version stays live.
Customers keep receiving answers from the previous version until the new
version is approved, at which point the old version is replaced rather
than archived.

Indexing

Passages are created by the nightly knowledge indexing job, which
consumes automation credits. An article approved during the day is
therefore not retrievable by GuideFlow until the next indexing run
completes, unless an administrator triggers a manual reindex. A manual
reindex of a single article costs 5 automation credits; a full reindex
of the Knowledge Hub costs 1 credit per article.

The indexing job splits each article into passages at headings first,
then at paragraph breaks. A passage is limited to roughly 800
characters. Tables are kept inside a single passage when possible,
because splitting a table separates values from their column headings.

The index snapshot is the frozen copy of passages that GuideFlow
searches. A new index snapshot is created after every indexing run, and
the previous snapshot is kept for 7 days so an administrator can roll
back if a bad article was published. An index snapshot is unrelated to
the daily billing snapshot.

Retrieval and filters

GuideFlow uses semantic retrieval to find passages related in meaning to
a query. It also applies metadata filters before final ranking. Filters
can exclude archived articles, restrict results to a product area, or
require a particular language. Semantic similarity alone therefore does
not guarantee that a passage is eligible.

Suppose Passage A has a semantic similarity of 0.84 but belongs to an
Archived article. Passage B has a similarity of 0.79 and belongs to an
Approved article that satisfies every filter. Passage B can be selected
even though its raw similarity score is lower. This is why similarity
scores should not be treated as direct quality scores for the entire
retrieval strategy.

Editors can attach synonyms to a collection. Synonyms are applied to the
query before retrieval, so a collection with the synonym seat = license
lets a customer who asks about licenses retrieve passages that only use
the word seat. Synonyms apply only to the collection where they are
defined.

Search results include article and passage identifiers. Passage
identifiers are stable within a published article version but may change
after substantial republishing. An evaluation system should therefore
avoid treating a passage identifier as a permanent global identifier.

Editors can mark articles as related. Related-article links help human
navigation, but GuideFlow does not automatically retrieve every related
article. Each article still has to earn a place in retrieval based on
the query and eligibility rules.

Writing guidelines

Good knowledge articles answer one primary question or a tightly
connected group of questions. Editors are encouraged to place
definitions near the beginning, procedures in numbered steps, and
exceptions in a clearly labeled section. This structure helps both human
readers and retrieval systems.

Procedural order matters. Imagine a billing adjustment procedure that
says the agent must verify the invoice before requesting approval.
Retrieving only the approval step can produce an incomplete answer even
if the retrieved passage contains the correct approval words. A
retrieval system should ideally preserve enough surrounding context to
reconstruct the complete procedure.

Article freshness

Every Approved article has a review date. The default review date is 180
days after approval. When the review date passes, the article stays
Approved and remains retrievable, but it is flagged as stale in the
Knowledge Hub and its owner is notified. GuideFlow lowers the ranking of
stale passages by a fixed penalty of 0.05 but never excludes them
because of staleness alone.
