AtlasDesk billing is based on active seats and optional automation
credits. A seat represents a licensed support user. Automation credits
represent usage of selected background jobs such as transcript
classification and nightly knowledge indexing. Normal message retrieval
does not consume automation credits. A workspace can therefore have many
active seats and zero automation credits consumed, or few active seats
and substantial automation usage.

An invoice contains subscription subtotal, automation usage,
adjustments, and total due. The subscription subtotal is based on
billable seats. Automation usage is based on credits consumed during the
billing period. Adjustments include approved credits or one-time
charges. The total due is calculated after applicable taxes.

A seat becomes billable when it is active at the end of the workspace’s
daily billing snapshot. Deactivating a seat immediately prevents
sign-in, but it does not retroactively remove the seat from a snapshot
that has already been recorded. If the user is later reactivated, the
system creates a new active seat record for the next snapshot.

Invoices are finalized at 02:00 UTC on the first day of the new billing
period. Finalization and the payment deadline are separate events.
Finalization creates the accounting record for the previous period. The
payment deadline is the date by which the resulting balance must be
paid. Changing the payment method does not move either time.

Failed payments use a three-stage recovery process. The first failed
attempt creates a warning and schedules another attempt after 24 hours.
A second failure schedules a third attempt after another 48 hours. If
the third attempt fails, the workspace enters a restricted billing
state. Existing tickets remain readable, but new automation jobs are
paused until the balance is resolved.

The billing-review workflow begins with invoice identification. The
agent records the invoice number and billing period, then compares the
disputed line item with the corresponding billing record. If the line
item is correct, the agent explains the calculation. If it is incorrect,
the agent documents the discrepancy and requests an adjustment. A
finalized invoice cannot be edited directly; corrections appear as
adjustment entries on a later invoice.

Some support requests combine multiple workflows. If a customer cannot
access an account and also disputes a charge, the agent should not
assume that one workflow solves both problems. The access problem
follows identity verification and account recovery. The billing problem
follows invoice review. One ticket may contain both issues.

Whenever a workflow requires approval, the approval must happen before
an agent promises that the requested change will be made. This applies
to refunds, billing adjustments, account exceptions, and retention
changes. A request can be likely to be approved without being approved
yet.
